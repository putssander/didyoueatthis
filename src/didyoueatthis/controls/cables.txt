SUBJECT: MINISTRY SIGNALS FLEXIBILITY ON PORT CONCESSION TALKS 1. (C) Summary. In a January 14 meeting, Deputy Transport Minister Halvard Rensen told Ambassador that the government is prepared to reopen the terms of the northern port concession if the consortium accepts a shorter initial term and a revenue-sharing clause indexed to container volume. Rensen characterized the cabinet as divided, with the finance ministry favoring a quick close and the prime minister's office wary of a domestic backlash before the spring elections. He asked that the embassy convey to the consortium that a formal counterproposal would be welcome before the end of the month. End summary. 2. (C) Rensen was noticeably more forthcoming than in December, when he had insisted that the concession terms were not negotiable. He attributed the shift to the collapse of the rival bid from the regional operator and to pressure from the port workers' union, which has threatened a work stoppage if the concession is awarded without guarantees on staffing levels. Comment: Rensen's willingness to engage suggests the government sees the consortium as the only credible bidder and is looking for a face-saving formula. Post recommends a low-key response that keeps the initiative with the ministry. End comment.

SUBJECT: OPPOSITION LEADER PREVIEWS ELECTION STRATEGY, SEEKS WESTERN ENGAGEMENT 1. (C) Summary. Opposition leader Teodora Vasquez told Poloff over dinner on March 3 that her coalition will campaign on anti-corruption and energy prices, and will avoid direct attacks on the president's foreign policy in order to keep moderate voters in play. She asked for a visible program of engagement with Western embassies, including invitations to national-day receptions, arguing that the government has used the opposition's isolation as evidence of its unreliability. End summary. 2. (C) Vasquez was candid about the coalition's weaknesses: the two rural parties remain suspicious of her urban base, and fundraising has been hampered by the new law requiring disclosure of donors above a modest threshold. She expects the government to schedule the vote for the earliest permissible date to limit her organizing time. 3. (C) Comment. Vasquez is the most plausible alternative to the current government, but her coalition is fragile and her request for embassy engagement carries obvious risks of appearing to take sides. Post will continue routine contacts at the working level and recommends against high-visibility gestures before the election date is announced. End comment.

SUBJECT: CENTRAL BANK GOVERNOR ON RESERVE DRAWDOWN AND IMF CONTACTS 1. (SBU) Summary. Central Bank Governor Ilse Marquand told Econoff on June 9 that foreign reserves have fallen to roughly four months of import cover and that the bank has quietly opened technical discussions with the Fund on a precautionary arrangement. She stressed that no formal request has been made and asked that the contacts not be publicized, since the government fears a run on the currency if markets conclude that a program is imminent. End summary. 2. (SBU) Marquand blamed the drawdown on the fuel subsidy, which the government has refused to cut before the harvest season, and on the delayed disbursement of a bilateral loan. She expects the subsidy debate to come to a head in the September budget, and said the bank would raise the policy rate by at least a full point if the currency slides further. 3. (SBU) Comment. Marquand's remarks are the clearest signal yet that the authorities recognize the seriousness of the external position. Her request for discretion is reasonable, and post recommends that Washington interlocutors avoid public comment on the reserve figures. End comment.

SUBJECT: PROVINCIAL GOVERNOR DESCRIBES SECURITY SITUATION AFTER HIGHWAY ATTACKS 1. (C) Summary. Governor Amadou Sissako told the Ambassador on April 22 that the three attacks on the eastern highway in the past month were carried out by a splinter group that has broken with the main insurgent front over the distribution of ransom payments. He said provincial forces have identified the group's base camp but lack the airlift to reach it before the rains, and asked whether the embassy could expedite delivery of the communications equipment approved last year. End summary. 2. (C) Sissako was frank about the limits of his authority: the army units in the province report to the capital, and the regional commander has declined to share intelligence with provincial police. He described relations with the commander as correct but distant. 3. (C) Comment. Sissako's account of the splinter group matches reporting from other contacts, and the request for the communications equipment is reasonable. Post recommends approval, with the caveat that delivery be coordinated with the army to avoid another dispute over control of the network. End comment.

SUBJECT: LABOR FEDERATION WARNS OF GENERAL STRIKE OVER PENSION REFORM 1. (SBU) Summary. Labor federation president Jonas Kettering told Laboff on October 2 that the federation's executive council has authorized a general strike if the pension bill passes its second reading without amendments raising the minimum benefit and delaying the increase in the retirement age. He expects the vote within two weeks and said the federation has been in contact with the transport and dockworkers' unions, whose participation would shut down the ports. End summary. 2. (SBU) Kettering acknowledged that the federation's leverage has declined as membership has fallen, but argued that the government cannot afford a port shutdown during the export season. He claimed that several governing-party deputies from port cities have privately promised to support the amendments. 3. (SBU) Comment. A general strike remains a threat rather than a plan, but the port unions' involvement would make it consequential. Post will report on the second reading. End comment.

SUBJECT: FOREIGN MINISTRY READOUT OF BORDER COMMISSION SESSION 1. (C) Summary. Foreign Ministry Director for Neighboring States Marit Eklund provided Poloff on July 17 with a readout of the eleventh session of the joint border commission. The two sides agreed on the demarcation of the river sector but remain apart on the highland pastures, where the neighboring delegation insisted on a formula based on the 1931 survey that Eklund's government considers unreliable. The next session is scheduled for November. End summary. 2. (C) Eklund said her minister is under pressure from highland deputies not to concede any grazing land and would prefer to leave the sector undemarcated rather than accept the 1931 line. She asked whether the embassy could identify a neutral survey organization acceptable to both sides. 3. (C) Comment. The river agreement is genuine progress, and the request for a neutral survey is a constructive way to defer the highland question. Post recommends a positive response. End comment.
