What we found

We asked language models to continue public-domain novels word for word, to fill in a blanked name, to pick the real wording among rewrites, and to complete canary strings planted in public datasets years ago. Text written last week was the control. Here is what came back, in plain terms, as of 8 September 2026.

What the providers appear to do

None of this is confirmed by the companies. It is what the behaviour is consistent with, matched to what the research literature describes.

1. Train some models to decline recitation, and not others

Whether a model recites is a per-model choice, not a trend. GPT-3.5, GPT-4, GPT-4.1, o3, GPT-5.1, GPT-5.4, GPT-5.5 and GPT-5.6 Sol recite public-domain novels from a 32-word prefix; GPT-4o, GPT-4o mini, GPT-5, GPT-5 mini, GPT-5.2, GPT-5.6 Terra and GPT-6 Astra mostly answer “[UNKNOWN]” to the identical request, even though the text is legal to reproduce and everything else about them says it is memorised. Gemini Flash recites; Claude Fable 5.1, tested by hand, recites too. What it means for you: a refusal says something about the model’s tuning and nothing about whether your document was used; test on a model that recites the known positive.

2. Check rights at answer time

When asked for its reasoning, Gemini writes things like “the system allows verbatim text if it’s public domain; Pride and Prejudice was published in 1813” before reciting, and GPT-5 sometimes quotes a passage in its reasoning and then answers “[UNKNOWN]”. The decision to recite is made after the memory is retrieved. What it means for you: the reasoning summary, where a provider returns one, is the most honest window you get.

3. Filter benchmark canaries, sometimes

Evaluation datasets carry a canary string so trainers can exclude them. Gemini 3.8 Flash reproduces the BIG-bench canary in full, so the files or their many copies were not filtered out of its data. OpenAI models decline the same prompt, which given point 1 tells us nothing either way. What it means for you: a canary in your own document is the one test with a real answer; this site carries one.

4. Deduplicate and cut off

Text seen once is rarely extractable; text seen thousands of times is (the research is consistent on this). Nothing written after a model’s training cutoff was reproduced by any model, and no model completed the canary planted in May 2026. What it means for you: a document that existed in one place is unlikely to show up even if it was used; a widely copied one will.

5. Keep token probabilities and fine-tuning out of reach

The two methods that see through refusals most directly, scoring a text’s token probabilities and fine-tuning the model on a harmless task to reactivate recall, are no longer offered on OpenAI’s API as of 2026 and never were on the others. They remain possible with open-weight models run locally. What it means for you: the strongest tests now require a local model.

Read this before quoting a number

Sample sizes are small (4 to 16 passages per model), chosen to keep the study cheap; intervals in the full reports are wide. A reproduced passage proves exposure to the text, not which copy or which training stage. A missing reproduction proves nothing. Reasoning summaries are written by the provider and are often empty. Full method, limits and per-run tables: the techniques guide, the README and the canary page.
